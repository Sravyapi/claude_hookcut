A single 60-minute YouTube video contains dozens of potential Shorts. Most of them are bad. The challenge isn't finding enough clips — it's finding the *right* clips, the ones that work as standalone content in a feed where viewers decide to swipe in under two seconds.

This guide walks through the complete workflow — from selecting which videos to repurpose, to identifying hook moments, to producing and posting a batch of Shorts that actually perform.

---

## Step 1: Select the Right Source Videos

Not every long-form video is worth repurposing. The best source videos share three characteristics:

**High information density**: Videos where the creator makes specific, quotable points — not rambling conversations or vague motivational content. A 60-minute interview where the guest makes 20 distinct, interesting claims is better source material than a 60-minute tutorial with one idea stretched thin.

**Strong vocal delivery**: Shorts need energy. If the original video has a flat, monotone delivery, even the best hook moment will underperform because short-form viewers expect dynamic presentation.

**Standalone moments**: The video needs moments that make sense without context. If every interesting point requires 5 minutes of setup to understand, those moments won't work as 30-second Shorts.

Look at your analytics. Your longest-performing videos — the ones with the best audience retention — tend to have the most hookable moments, because the content was engaging enough to keep viewers watching in the first place.

---

## Step 2: Identify Hook Moments

This is the step that determines everything. A Strong Short starts with a strong hook — and finding those hooks in 60 minutes of content is the most time-consuming part of the manual workflow.

### The Manual Approach

Watch the video at 1.5-2x speed with a timestamp log open. Flag every moment where:

- The speaker makes a surprising claim or shares an unexpected statistic
- The energy level spikes — vocal emphasis, emotional intensity, or dramatic pacing
- A story begins with specific, concrete details
- The speaker contradicts conventional wisdom
- A question is posed that the audience would genuinely want answered
- The speaker articulates a pain point with unusual precision

For a 60-minute video, expect to identify 15-25 potential hook moments. Most won't make the final cut — but you need a pool to select from.

### What Makes a Good Hook Moment

The hook moment isn't necessarily the most interesting thing said in the video. It's the moment most likely to stop a scroll — which is a different thing entirely.

A brilliant insight delivered with flat energy won't stop anyone. A moderately interesting claim delivered with conviction and surprise will. The hook needs to work *visually and auditorily in two seconds*, before the viewer has any context about who's speaking or what the topic is.

Good hook moments typically share these traits:
- Start mid-energy (no throat-clearing or setup)
- Contain a specific, concrete detail in the first sentence
- Create an implicit question or curiosity gap
- Feel complete enough to stand alone but open enough to pull the viewer forward

## Step 3: Clip and Structure Each Short

Once you've identified your hook moments, the clipping process follows a consistent structure.

### The Three-Part Short

Most high-performing Shorts follow a simple structure:

**Hook (0-3 seconds)**: The moment that stops the scroll. This is the hook moment you identified — the surprising claim, the shocking statistic, the emotionally resonant statement.

**Body (3-25 seconds)**: The development of the hook's promise. If the hook posed a question, the body answers it. If the hook made a claim, the body supports it. The body needs to reward the viewer for staying past the hook.

**Payoff (final 3-5 seconds)**: A conclusion that feels satisfying — a punchline, a summary, a call-to-action, or a restatement of the key insight. The payoff prevents the "so what?" feeling that causes viewers to leave without engaging.

### Clip Length

Aim for 20-60 seconds per Short. Under 15 seconds rarely provides enough value to drive meaningful engagement. Over 60 seconds requires exceptional retention to justify the length.

The content dictates the length. A single surprising statistic with a quick explanation might be 20 seconds. A mini-story with a twist might need 50. Don't pad short clips or truncate long ones — let the natural rhythm of the content determine the boundaries.

### Trimming Boundaries

Where you start and end the clip matters enormously. Starting one sentence too early — with a transitional phrase or setup context — kills the hook. Ending one sentence too late — with a trailing "so, yeah" or topic transition — weakens the payoff.

Trim aggressively. The first frame of the Short should be the first syllable of the hook. The last frame should be the final word of the payoff.

## Step 4: Add Captions

Captions aren't optional for Shorts. A significant percentage of viewers watch with sound off, and even viewers with sound on process captioned content differently — captions increase comprehension and retention.

### Caption Style Matters

The style of your captions affects perceived quality and brand consistency:

**Clean**: White text, minimal styling. Professional and readable. Works for educational and business content.

**Bold**: Large, high-contrast text with emphasis coloring on key words. Works for motivational, entertainment, and high-energy content.

**Neon**: Stylized captions with glow effects. Works for creative and entertainment content targeting younger audiences.

**Minimal**: Small, unobtrusive captions at the bottom of the frame. Works for cinematic or visual-first content where captions shouldn't compete with the imagery.

Pick one style and use it consistently across all your Shorts. Caption style is part of your brand identity on the Shorts shelf.

## Step 5: Batch Production Workflow

Turning one video into 10 Shorts is efficient only if you batch the work. Switching between tasks — watching, clipping, captioning, exporting — is where time gets wasted.

### The Efficient Sequence

1. **Watch and timestamp** (30-45 minutes for a 60-minute video at 1.5x)
2. **Select top 10 hooks** from your timestamp log (10 minutes)
3. **Clip all 10** in sequence (30-40 minutes)
4. **Add captions to all 10** in sequence (20-30 minutes)
5. **Export all 10** (5-10 minutes)
6. **Write titles and descriptions for all 10** (15-20 minutes)

Total time for the manual workflow: roughly 2-3 hours per long-form video, producing 10 Shorts.

---

## Step 6: Posting Strategy

Don't post all 10 Shorts at once. Spread them across your posting schedule — 2-3 per week is a sustainable cadence for most channels.

**Sequence strategically**: Lead with your strongest hook. If your first Short from a batch performs well, the algorithm gives favorable initial distribution to subsequent Shorts from your channel.

**Test different hooks from the same video**: If a video has multiple strong moments, test which hook types perform best. A curiosity gap hook and a shock statistic hook from the same interview will reach different audiences and perform differently.

**Cross-promote**: In the description of each Short, link back to the full video. Viewers who discover you through a Short and then watch your long-form content send strong signals to the algorithm.

**Track performance per hook type**: Over time, you'll develop a data-backed understanding of which hook types work best for your audience. This data compounds — each batch of Shorts refines your future hook selection.

## Manual vs. AI-Assisted Approaches

The workflow described above is entirely manual. It works, but it's time-intensive — 2-3 hours per video, with the quality heavily dependent on your ability to identify strong hook moments.

AI-assisted approaches automate the most time-consuming steps:

**Transcript analysis**: Instead of watching the full video at 1.5x, AI analyzes the transcript and identifies hook candidates in seconds.

**Hook scoring**: Instead of relying on your subjective judgment about which moments are strongest, AI scores each candidate across multiple engagement dimensions — curiosity, specificity, emotional arousal, forward pull.

**Clip generation**: Instead of manually trimming each clip in an editor, AI generates clips with precise boundaries aligned to the hook moment.

**Caption rendering**: Instead of manually styling and timing captions, AI renders captions in your chosen style directly onto the clip.

The tradeoff is control. Manual workflows give you complete creative control over every decision. AI-assisted workflows trade some of that control for speed — turning a 2-3 hour process into a 10-minute one.

The ideal approach for most creators is hybrid: let AI handle the identification and initial clipping, then review and adjust before posting. This captures most of the time savings while maintaining creative oversight.
